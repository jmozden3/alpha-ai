## ⚡ The Alpha — Tip of the Week

**Make AI write in your voice by interviewing you first — not by asking it to "sound like me."**

Alex Lieberman, who founded Morning Brew, built a content workflow with Claude that never produces generic slop. The trick isn't a magic prompt — it's flipping the order. Instead of asking AI to draft something and then fixing the voice, he makes the AI **interview him before it writes a single word.**

Here's how to steal this for your next post, email, or update:

1. **Start by telling the AI to interview you.** Paste this: *"I want to write a [LinkedIn post / client update / newsletter] about [topic]. Before you write anything, ask me 5–7 questions one at a time to pull out my real opinions, specific examples, and how I'd naturally say this. Wait for each answer before the next question."*
2. **Answer like you're talking to a colleague** — messy, specific, with real anecdotes. This is where your voice actually lives.
3. **Then have it draft** using only your answers, and ask it to keep your phrasing wherever possible.
4. **Run one revision pass:** *"Now cut anything that sounds like generic corporate writing or a press release. Keep it in my words."*

The reason this works: most AI writing sounds like slop because you gave it a topic and no *you*. The interview step forces your actual thinking onto the page before the AI smooths it into mush. Try it once before your next post and you'll never go back to "write me a LinkedIn post about X."*Source: Lenny's Newsletter — https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-the-founder-of-morning-brew-built*## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**ChatGPT for Small Businesses** — OpenAI's new program aimed at entrepreneurs who want to automate everyday work without hiring a consultant or learning to code.

If you run a small business, here's how to put it to use today:

1. Open ChatGPT and pick one recurring, time-sucking task — writing customer replies, drafting invoices, summarizing your week's sales notes.
2. Describe the task in plain English, then paste in a real example of how you currently do it.
3. Ask ChatGPT to turn it into a repeatable step-by-step process you can reuse — and save that as a note you paste back in each week.

Most useful for: SMB owners and solo operators who feel like they're drowning in admin and want AI handling the repetitive stuff so they can focus on customers.*Source: OpenAI — https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-small-business-program*## 💡 Prompt of the Week

Before you agree to build, buy, or launch anything, pressure-test whether people actually want it — a lesson straight from watching founders ship four products that all quietly died. Use this when you're excited about a new idea and tempted to skip validation:

```
I'm considering [describe your idea or project]. Act as a skeptical
customer discovery coach. First, list the 5 riskiest assumptions I'm
making about whether people actually want this. Then, for each one,
give me a specific question I could ask 5 real potential customers to
test it — questions that don't lead them toward saying yes. Finally,
tell me what answer would be a red flag that I should stop.
```

This forces the annoying-but-essential step — talking to real people — to the front, before you sink a weekend (or a budget) into something nobody asked for.*Source: r/AI_Agents — https://www.reddit.com/r/AI_Agents/comments/1v2hgu4/ai_didnt_make_software_development_cheap_it_made/*## 📡 Signal vs Noise
