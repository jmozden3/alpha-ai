## ⚡ The Alpha

**How to audit what AI actually "knows" about your work in 15 minutes**

Most people paste things into Claude or ChatGPT and trust whatever comes back. But there's a smarter move: before asking AI to help with anything important, ask it to show you exactly what it's working with first.

Here's how to do it in Claude (works in ChatGPT too):

1. Open a conversation where you've shared documents, notes, or background info
2. Before asking your real question, type: *"Before we start, summarize what you know about my project, my goals, and any constraints I've mentioned. Flag anything that seems unclear or missing."*
3. Read what it gives back. You'll almost always spot something wrong — a misunderstood goal, a missing detail, or an assumption it quietly made
4. Correct those gaps, *then* ask your actual question

This takes about 15 minutes the first time you do it, and it dramatically cuts down on AI giving you confidently wrong answers. Think of it as checking that your GPS has the right destination before you start driving.

## 💡 Prompt of the Week

**The "AI Secretary" daily planning prompt**

This one turns any AI assistant into something that actually organizes your day — not just summarizes it. The trick is giving it your raw chaos (task lists, calendar blocks, random notes) and asking it to build a structured day around your actual energy and priorities.

Use it first thing in the morning. Paste in whatever you have — a messy to-do list, meeting times, a few notes — and let it build your day.

```
I need you to act as my daily planning secretary. Here is my raw input for today:

TASKS: [paste your to-do list or brain dump here]
MEETINGS/FIXED COMMITMENTS: [paste any scheduled blocks]
ENERGY NOTE: [e.g., "I'm sharpest in the morning" or "I have a 2pm slump"]
ONE PRIORITY: [the single most important thing that must happen today]

Please:
1. Build a realistic hour-by-hour schedule for my workday
2. Put my ONE PRIORITY in my best energy window
3. Group similar tasks together to reduce context switching
4. Flag anything that looks like it won't fit and suggest what to cut or defer
5. End with one sentence: what does a successful version of today look like?
```

**Why it works:** Most people use AI reactively. This flips it — you're using it proactively to design your day before the day designs itself.

*Source: The Rundown AI*

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Turn your messy meeting notes into a structured business report in under 5 minutes**

If you're regularly sending updates, writing summaries, or reporting on projects, this will save you real time every week.

Here's exactly how:

1. After any meeting, take 2 minutes to jot raw notes — doesn't have to be pretty. Just capture what was said, decided, and who's doing what
2. Open Claude.ai or ChatGPT
3. Paste your notes and add this instruction at the top:

   *"Turn these raw meeting notes into a professional summary report. Include: a 2-sentence executive summary, key decisions made, action items with owners and deadlines (infer reasonable deadlines if not stated), and any open questions that still need answers. Keep it scannable with headers."*

4. Read the output, make any corrections, send

The key insight here — borrowed from one experienced builder this week — is that the interface matters as much as the AI itself. A good prompt structure is your interface. The AI is capable; it just needs you to set it up right.

This works for project updates, client recaps, team standups, or any situation where you're turning a conversation into a document.

**🔇 NOISE: AI "agents" rolling out at companies**

You're going to see a lot of coverage this week about "AI agents" — AI systems that can take actions autonomously, browse the web, send emails, manage tasks without you watching every step. A new study found that 74% of companies have already deployed these agents in some form.

Here's why you can ignore this for now: that same study found that 74% of those deployments were also rolled back or shut down after going live. The main reason? Nobody could tell what the agent actually did or why, which made them impossible to trust.

Agents are genuinely coming and will be useful eventually. But right now, even the companies with big teams and budgets are struggling to make them work reliably. You don't need to learn agent tools this week. Focus on using the chat-based AI you already have — there's still enormous untapped value there for most people.

Check back in on agents in six months.
