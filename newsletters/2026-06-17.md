## ⚡ The Alpha — Tip of the Week

**Turn AI into your scope-protection lawyer before you say yes to anything**

Scope creep is where projects go to die. You agree to one thing, then three weeks later you're doing five things, and somehow you're the one who feels bad about asking to be paid for it.

Here's a habit worth building before your next project conversation: before you agree to anything — a new project, a client request, a cross-team favor — open ChatGPT or Claude and describe what you've been asked to do in plain language. Then send this prompt:

*"I've been asked to [describe the request]. Write a one-page scope document that defines exactly what's included, what's explicitly NOT included, three ways this project typically expands beyond the original ask, and a list of questions I should get answered before agreeing."*

What comes back is a working draft of a scope document plus a built-in warning system. You review it, tighten it, and either send it to the client or use it to anchor your conversation. The questions it surfaces — "Who signs off on final approval?" "What happens if requirements change?" — are the ones most people forget to ask until it's too late.

One freelancer in the r/AI_Agents community described exactly this pain: a $2,000 project that ate six weeks of revisions because scope wasn't defined upfront. A scope document sent before the work starts isn't bureaucracy — it's self-defense.

Takes five minutes. Do it before the next thing you agree to.

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**Canva + ChatGPT image export**

If you've ever used ChatGPT to generate an image — a diagram, a slide visual, a social post mockup — you may not have noticed that you can now send that image directly into Canva as an editable design. That means the AI-generated visual becomes a starting point you can actually modify: swap fonts, recolor elements, add your logo, resize for different formats.

**How to use it right now:**
1. Generate an image in ChatGPT as you normally would
2. Look for the option to export or open the image in Canva (available from the image output)
3. In Canva, the image opens as an editable file — not a flat picture you have to work around

Most useful for: anyone who creates presentations, social content, or client-facing visuals and wants to go from "AI-generated rough" to "actually polished" without starting over in a design tool.

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Use the "gardening" loop to finally get more out of AI on big projects**

Most people use AI like a vending machine: put in a prompt, get out an answer, done. That works fine for one-off tasks. But for anything complex — a project you're managing over weeks, a recurring report, a big decision — there's a better model.

Think of it as gardening rather than vending. Here's what it looks like in practice:

1. **Start a dedicated chat** for a specific project (not a new chat every time)
2. **Give AI the full context once** — the goal, the constraints, who the stakeholders are, what's already been decided
3. **At the end of each work session**, paste in what changed, what you learned, what's still unresolved, and ask: *"Given this update, what should I be thinking about or doing next?"*
4. **Feed the output back in** next session — AI picks up where you left off

The difference between people who feel like AI is transformative and people who find it marginal often isn't the tool — it's whether they're doing one-shot prompts or running an ongoing, informed loop. The loop is where the leverage is.

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**SIGNAL: Sixty percent of consumers say "AI" in brand messaging is a turnoff**

A new study found that roughly 60% of US consumers say seeing "AI" prominently in a product's marketing makes them less likely to buy it — and this matters to you directly if you're writing copy, pitching clients, or positioning anything at work. Lead with what the thing *does for the person*, not the technology behind it.
