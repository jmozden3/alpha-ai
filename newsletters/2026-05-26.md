## ⚡ The Alpha

**Stop writing your own emails. But also — stop letting AI write them for you.**

This week, Paul Graham (the entrepreneur and investor) made a pointed observation that's worth paying attention to: he can tell when emails are AI-written, and when he figures it out, he stops reading. His words: "It feels like being lied to."

Here's the actionable flip side — use AI to *improve* your writing, not replace it. The difference is subtle but important.

**How to do it:**

1. Write your email, message, or update yourself first — even if it's rough and bullet-point style
2. Paste it into Claude or ChatGPT with this instruction: *"Make this tighter and clearer, but keep my voice and sentence structure. Don't make it sound polished or corporate."*
3. Read the output out loud. If it doesn't sound like you, ask it to pull back: *"Too formal — make it sound more like a person wrote it quickly."*
4. Do one final check: would you be comfortable if the recipient knew you used AI? If yes, send it.

The goal is AI as editor, not ghostwriter. You get the clarity boost without the uncanny-valley effect that's making smart people tune out AI-written messages entirely.

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**ElevenReader (iPhone app)**

ElevenReader turns any article, document, or long web page into high-quality audio — narrated in a voice that sounds genuinely human — while highlighting each paragraph as it reads.

**How to use it right now:**

1. Download ElevenReader from the App Store (it's free to get started)
2. Find any long article, report, or document you've been meaning to read — grab its URL
3. Open ElevenReader and paste the URL directly in
4. Hit play, put your phone in your pocket, and listen on your commute, walk, or gym session

That's it. No copying and pasting text. No formatting fuss. Simon Willison used it to listen through an entire papal encyclical on a dog walk this week, which is honestly a pretty solid endorsement.

**Best for:** Anyone drowning in reading backlogs — long reports, research, newsletters, articles you've had open in a tab for three weeks.

## 💡 Prompt of the Week

**The "Audit My Work" Prompt**

One underused move: asking AI to review a piece of your work the way a sharp outside critic would — not to rewrite it, but to find the gaps you've stopped seeing. The Rundown AI flagged this approach this week as a 15-minute exercise worth building into your routine.

Here's the exact prompt. Paste your document, report, email, or plan after it:

```
You are a sharp, honest colleague reviewing my work before it goes to a client or decision-maker. Your job is not to rewrite anything — just to audit it.

Tell me:
1. What's the single weakest part of this, and why?
2. What assumption am I making that I haven't justified?
3. What question would a skeptical reader ask that I haven't answered?
4. Is anything unclear that I might be too close to notice?

Be direct. Don't soften your feedback.

Here's the work:
[paste your document here]
```

**When to use it:** Before sending a proposal, presenting a plan, publishing anything, or handing off a project. It takes about two minutes and consistently catches the thing you assumed was obvious but isn't.

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Turn a case study or project result into a short client-ready video — no camera, no editing skills required**

The Rundown AI highlighted this workflow this week, and it's genuinely useful for anyone who needs to communicate results to clients, stakeholders, or a team without spending hours on production.

**Here's how to do it:**

1. **Write up your case study in plain text** — three paragraphs is enough. Problem, what you did, result.
2. **Open ChatGPT or Claude and paste it in** with this instruction: *"Turn this into a tight 60-second video script. Use short sentences. Start with the result, then explain how we got there. End with one clear takeaway."*
3. **Take that script into a tool like Synthesia or HeyGen** (both have free tiers) — these tools generate a talking-head video from text. You pick an AI presenter, paste the script, and it renders a video in minutes.
4. **Download and share** — it works as a client update, a LinkedIn post, or an internal team recap.

No camera. No editing. No waiting on a video team. If you have a result worth sharing, you can have a polished short video in under 30 minutes.

*Source: The Rundown AI — https://www.therundown.ai/p/the-pope-just-weighed-in-on-ai*

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**SIGNAL: AI tools are getting much better at running tasks on your behalf — and it's starting to matter for how you manage your work**

A theme running through multiple sources this week: AI is moving from "answer my question" to "go handle this thing for me." Anthropic released new features for what they're calling managed agents — AI that can run tasks in loops, learn from previous sessions, and work in parallel on multiple subtasks simultaneously. Google made similar moves at their developer conference this week.

Why does this matter to you right now, even if you're not technical? Because the tools you already use — Claude, ChatGPT, and the apps built on top of them — are starting to reflect this shift. Features that let AI draft, research, revise, and follow up across a whole project (not just answer one question) are landing in consumer products.

The practical upshot: if you haven't tried giving AI a *multi-step* task recently — something like "research three options for X, compare them, and draft a recommendation" rather than just "what is X?" — this week is a good time to try. The gap between what you think these tools can do and what they can actually do has widened considerably.

**NOISE: AI security exploits in enterprise software**

This week a security researcher found that Microsoft's Copilot Cowork product had a vulnerability where malicious content could trick the AI into leaking your files to an outside attacker — via a technique called prompt injection.

This sounds alarming, but here's why you can set it aside for now: this specific issue is in an enterprise product most individuals don't use, Microsoft is aware and working on it, and this class of problem is actively being addressed across the industry. It's a real and important challenge for the people building these systems — but it doesn't require any action from you today, and following it closely won't change anything about how you use AI tools in your daily work.

File it under "things the engineers need to solve" and check back in six months.
