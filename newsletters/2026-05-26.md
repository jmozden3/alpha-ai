## ⚡ The Alpha

**Turn Claude into a 15-minute audit of your own work**

Most people use AI to *create* things. This week's most underrated move is using it to *review* what you've already done — and catch the gaps you're too close to see.

Here's how to do it: Open Claude (claude.ai — free to use). Paste in any piece of work you've produced this week: a proposal, a project update, a set of meeting notes, a client email thread. Then give it this instruction:

*"Read everything I've just shared and tell me: What assumptions am I making that I haven't stated out loud? What questions would a skeptical reader ask that I haven't answered? What's the weakest part of my argument or plan?"*

Give it 15 minutes. The goal isn't to have Claude rewrite your work — it's to use it as a tough-but-patient reader who has no stake in making you feel good. You'll surface blind spots faster than you would in a week of internal review.

This works on anything: strategy docs, client pitches, performance reviews, product briefs. The longer and more complex the document, the more useful this becomes.

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**ElevenReader — have AI read anything to you, hands-free**

ElevenReader is an iPhone app that takes any article, document, or URL you paste in and reads it aloud to you in a high-quality AI voice, highlighting each paragraph as it goes. Think of it as a podcast version of anything you'd normally have to sit down and read.

**How to use it right now:**
1. Download ElevenReader from the iPhone App Store (it's free)
2. Find something you've been meaning to read — a long report, a newsletter, a research paper — and copy its URL
3. Open ElevenReader, paste in the URL
4. Hit play. It reads the whole thing while you walk, commute, or do dishes

It works especially well for longer documents — the kind you keep meaning to get to but never quite do. Simon Willison used it to get through the Pope's full encyclical on AI during a dog walk, and said the quality was notably good.

**Best for:** Busy professionals with a backlog of reading, or anyone who absorbs information better by listening than by staring at a screen.

## 💡 Prompt of the Week

**The "Skeptical Manager" review prompt**

One of the most useful things AI can do is play a role you choose — specifically, someone who has high standards and no reason to be polite. This prompt turns any AI assistant into a demanding-but-fair senior reviewer of your work before it goes out the door.

**When to use it:** Before sending a proposal, presenting a plan to leadership, publishing anything externally, or any time you want a second opinion without scheduling a meeting.

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude, or any AI assistant, then add your document below it:

```
You are a sharp, experienced senior manager reviewing this work before it goes to a client or executive audience. You have high standards and genuinely want this to succeed, but you will not soften your feedback to protect my feelings.

Please review what I've shared and give me:
1. The single biggest problem with this document — the one thing that could make it fall flat
2. Three specific questions a skeptical reader will ask that this doesn't currently answer
3. One concrete suggestion to make the opening stronger

Be direct. I'd rather hear hard feedback now than get it from the actual audience.

[Paste your document here]
```

The key mechanic here is giving the AI a clear role and explicit permission to be honest. Without that permission, most AI tools default to diplomatic softness that isn't nearly as useful.

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Build yourself an AI "daily briefing" in under 10 minutes**

Most people start their day reacting — to emails, to Slack, to whatever's loudest. Here's a way to use AI to get ahead of it instead.

**Here's the exact workflow:**

1. Each morning, spend 3 minutes writing a quick brain-dump into ChatGPT or Claude. It doesn't need to be organized. Just type out: what's on your plate today, what you're anxious about, what meetings you have, any outstanding decisions you're waiting on.

2. Then paste this instruction: *"Based on everything I've just told you, give me: the single most important thing I should finish before noon, the one decision I should stop waiting on and just make, and one thing I can drop or defer without it mattering."*

3. Read the output. Argue with it if you disagree — that argument is often where the real clarity comes.

The whole thing takes under 10 minutes and functions like a daily standup with a tireless, context-aware thought partner. It's most powerful when you do it consistently, because you'll start noticing patterns in what keeps appearing on your plate day after day.

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**SIGNAL: The Pope just weighed in on AI — and the framing is more useful than you'd expect**

This week, Pope Leo XIV released a full encyclical — essentially a major formal letter — on AI and its impact on human dignity, labor, and society. Setting aside any religious dimension, this matters to ordinary people for a specific reason: it's one of the clearest, most readable frameworks yet published for thinking about *what we should actually be worried about* with AI.

The document draws a deliberate parallel to the Industrial Revolution, framing AI as a similarly sweeping shift that requires society to think carefully about workers, fairness, and what gets lost when powerful technology moves faster than our institutions.

Why does this matter to you personally? Because these are the ethical questions that are going to shape regulation, workplace policy, and public opinion about AI over the next several years. Understanding the conversation — even at a high level — puts you ahead of colleagues who are only tracking the technical developments. If you want to actually read it, paste the Vatican News URL into ElevenReader (see Tool of the Week above) and listen on your next walk.

*Source: Simon Willison — https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/25/encyclical-on-ai/#atom-everything*

**NOISE: AI "solving" 80-year-old math problems**

You may have seen headlines this week about OpenAI's models disproving a long-standing mathematical conjecture. It's genuinely interesting to researchers. But for anyone who isn't a mathematician or AI scientist, this is firmly in the "cool, so what?" category for now.

The fact that AI can make progress on abstract mathematical problems does not translate into anything you can use differently at work next week. It tells us that frontier AI models are getting better at a very specific type of formal reasoning — but that capability has almost no overlap with the tasks most professionals actually need help with. When it does start mattering for your work, you'll know, because the tools themselves will change. Until then, you can safely scroll past these headlines.

*Source: The Neuron — https://www.theneurondaily.com/p/openai-solved-an-80-year-math-problem-by-disproving-it*
