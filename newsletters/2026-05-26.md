## ⚡ The Alpha

**Stop using AI to write your emails. Start using it to audit them.**

This week, writer Paul Graham made a point worth tattooing on your forehead: when someone realizes an email was written by AI, they stop reading it. Not because AI writing is bad — but because it doesn't sound like *you*, and people can tell.

Here's the flip: instead of asking AI to write your emails, use it to improve yours. Here's exactly how:

1. Write your email yourself, however rough
2. Paste it into ChatGPT or Claude
3. Type: *"What's unclear or weak in this email? Don't rewrite it — just tell me what to fix and why"*
4. Make the edits yourself, in your own voice

You keep your voice. You get sharper thinking. And nobody reading your email feels tricked.

This works for anything — client proposals, Slack messages, performance reviews. The AI becomes your editor, not your ghostwriter.

## 💡 Prompt of the Week

**The "Editor, Not Author" Prompt**

Most people use AI to generate content from scratch. The problem: it comes out sounding generic, and — as Paul Graham pointed out this week — people increasingly recognize and distrust it.

Instead, use this prompt to get AI feedback on your own writing without letting it take over your voice. Paste this directly into ChatGPT or Claude after your own draft:

```
I've written the text below. Do NOT rewrite it or suggest alternative versions. 
Instead, give me a numbered list of specific problems:
- What's unclear or confusing?
- What's missing that the reader probably wants to know?
- Where does the logic not hold up?
- Any sentences that are doing nothing?

Keep your feedback blunt and brief. Here's my text:

[paste your writing here]
```

**When to use it:** Before sending any important email, proposal, report summary, or message where you want to land well. You get the benefit of a sharp second reader without surrendering your voice to the machine.

*Source: Simon Willison (quoting Paul Graham) — https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/26/paul-graham/#atom-everything*

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Turn a boring case study into a client-ready video in minutes**

The Rundown AI flagged a workflow this week worth stealing: using AI to convert a written case study into a video you can actually send to prospects or clients.

Here's how to do it:

1. **Open a tool like Synthesia or HeyGen** (both have free tiers — no filming required, they use AI avatars)
2. **Take any case study, project summary, or success story** you've already written — even a rough one
3. **Paste it into ChatGPT or Claude first** with this instruction: *"Rewrite this as a 90-second spoken video script. One clear problem, one clear solution, one clear result. Conversational tone, no jargon."*
4. **Copy the script into Synthesia or HeyGen**, pick an avatar and voice, and generate the video
5. **Send it** as a follow-up to a prospect, add it to a proposal, or drop it on your LinkedIn

The whole thing takes under 30 minutes and looks far more polished than a wall of text. No camera, no editing software, no production team.

Most useful for: consultants, freelancers, small business owners, or anyone in sales who needs to communicate results quickly.

*Source: The Rundown AI — https://www.therundown.ai/p/the-pope-just-weighed-in-on-ai*

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**SIGNAL: AI is shifting from "chat with it" to "it goes and does things for you"**

Multiple sources this week — One Useful Thing, The Rundown AI, Latent Space, and Every — all pointed at the same thing from different angles: the AI industry is moving fast away from the chatbot model toward what's being called *agentic AI*, meaning AI that takes a task, goes off and works on it, and comes back with a finished result.

What does this mean for you, practically? The tools you use every day are about to change shape. Instead of typing a question and getting an answer, you'll assign a task — "draft and send the follow-up emails from yesterday's meeting," or "research these three vendors and give me a comparison" — and the AI will handle the whole chain of steps, not just one response.

Google announced a wave of these capabilities at Google I/O this week. Anthropic, OpenAI, and others are all moving the same direction.

You don't need to do anything about this today. But it's worth knowing the window for "learning how to prompt well" is being replaced by a new window: "learning how to delegate well to AI." The skill is shifting from asking good questions to giving clear instructions with clear outcomes — which, usefully, is just good management.

*Source: One Useful Thing — https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/management-as-ai-superpower*
