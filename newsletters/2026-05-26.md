## ⚡ The Alpha

**Turn Claude into a 15-minute audit of your own work**

Here's a trick that's flying under the radar: you can drop a big chunk of your own writing, project notes, or strategy doc into Claude and ask it to audit what it actually *understands* about your situation — not just summarize, but tell you what's clear, what's contradictory, and what's missing.

Here's how to do it right now:

1. Open [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) (free account works)
2. Paste in a work document — could be a project brief, a client proposal, meeting notes, anything you've been staring at too long
3. Type: *"Read this carefully. Tell me: what's the core goal here, what's ambiguous or contradictory, and what important context seems to be missing?"*
4. Read the response. Claude will often flag assumptions you didn't realize you'd made — or gaps a client/boss will definitely catch

This works especially well for anything you've written yourself, because you're too close to it to see the holes. Claude isn't. Budget 15 minutes. It's uncomfortable in a useful way.

*(Source: The Rundown AI)*

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**ElevenReader (iPhone app)**

ElevenReader turns any article, document, or web page into high-quality spoken audio — like having a calm, clear voice read your reading list to you while you walk the dog or do the dishes.

**How to use it right now:**
1. Download ElevenReader from the App Store (free)
2. Copy any article URL — a long newsletter, a PDF, a web page you've been meaning to read
3. Paste the URL into the app
4. Hit play — it reads the content aloud in a natural voice, highlighting each paragraph as it goes so you can follow along if you glance at your phone

**Best for:** Anyone with a growing "read later" pile, commuters, or people who absorb information better by listening than reading. One writer this week used it to get through the Pope's entire encyclical on AI during a walk with his dog — and actually finished it.

## 💡 Prompt of the Week

**The "Smart Friend" Explainer**

When you encounter something in AI news — or anywhere — that sounds important but makes your eyes glaze over, use this prompt. It forces the AI to explain it the way a knowledgeable friend would over lunch, not the way a press release would.

**When to use it:** Any time you read a headline you don't fully understand but feel like you probably should. Takes 30 seconds.

```
I just read that [paste the confusing thing here]. 

Explain this to me like I'm a smart, busy professional who doesn't have a technical background. 

Specifically: What is actually happening here? Why does it matter to normal people? And is there anything I should do differently because of it? Keep it under 200 words and use zero jargon — if you have to use a technical term, define it in plain English right after.
```

This week's perfect test case: paste in "OpenAI disproved an 80-year-old math conjecture called the Erdős planar unit distance problem." You'll get a clear answer in seconds rather than clicking through five confusing articles.

*(Source: The Neuron, Latent Space)*

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Build a personal AI secretary that plans your day**

Most people use AI reactively — they have a problem, they ask a question. This workflow flips it: you give an AI your raw inputs at the start of the day and let it hand you back a structured plan.

Here's the exact process:

1. Open ChatGPT or Claude at the start of your workday
2. Paste in a brain dump — your task list, any emails you're stressed about, your meetings for the day, and one sentence about your energy level ("I slept badly" or "I have a 3pm deadline looming")
3. Type this instruction: *"Based on everything above, give me a prioritized plan for today. Group tasks by what requires deep focus vs. what I can do while tired or distracted. Flag anything I should probably say no to or push to tomorrow."*
4. The AI will hand back a sequenced day — not just a reordered list, but one that accounts for cognitive load

The key move is including your energy level and any emotional context. Without that, it just reorders your to-do list. With it, it actually thinks about when you should do what.

*(Source: The Rundown AI)*

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**📶 SIGNAL: AI is getting dramatically better at doing things *for* you, not just talking to you**

The biggest quiet shift this week is one worth paying attention to: every major AI company is moving from "answer my question" to "go do the task." Anthropic just announced features that let AI agents run on their own, check their own work, and improve between sessions. Google made similar moves at their developer conference.

What this means for you practically: the AI tools you're using today will start feeling less like a search engine and more like a capable assistant who can actually *complete* things — draft and send an email, research a topic and write up a report, book something on your behalf. You don't need to do anything differently right now, but it's worth starting to think about which repetitive tasks in your week you'd hand off if you could. That list will become useful sooner than you think.

**🔇 NOISE: The math breakthrough headlines**

You've probably seen versions of "AI just solved an 80-year-old math problem!" this week. It's real, it's genuinely impressive to mathematicians, and it means absolutely nothing for how you work or live right now.

This is a research result — AI helping solve a specific, abstract problem in pure mathematics. It's not a new tool, it's not a feature you'll use, and it doesn't signal that AI is suddenly smarter at things you care about. File it under "interesting dinner party fact" and move on. When a math breakthrough turns into something you can actually use, we'll tell you.
