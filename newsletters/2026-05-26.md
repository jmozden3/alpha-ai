## ⚡ The Alpha

**Stop writing emails from scratch. Use AI to turn your rough thoughts into a finished message in 60 seconds.**

Here's the thing nobody tells you about AI and writing: the goal isn't to have AI write *for* you — it's to have AI write *with* your voice, so you stay in the driver's seat. But there's a specific failure mode to avoid, and this week Paul Graham put it bluntly: when someone can tell an email was written by AI, it actually damages your credibility. It "feels like being lied to," he wrote.

So here's how to use AI for email without it backfiring:

1. Open ChatGPT or Claude
2. Type your raw thoughts as if you're venting to a friend — messy, incomplete, whatever's in your head
3. Add this instruction at the top: *"Clean this up into a clear, direct email. Keep my voice. Don't add filler phrases or corporate language. Don't make it sound polished to the point of being fake."*
4. Read the draft out loud. If it doesn't sound like you, ask it to make specific changes: "make it shorter" or "make the opening less formal"
5. Send only when it passes the "would I actually say this?" test

The point isn't to outsource your communication — it's to stop staring at a blank screen. You bring the thinking, AI brings the structure.

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**ElevenReader — a free iPhone app that reads any article or document to you in a high-quality AI voice, highlighting along as it goes**

What it does: You paste in a URL (a news article, a PDF, a long report — anything), and it reads it to you out loud with a natural-sounding voice, highlighting each paragraph in real time so you can follow along.

**How to use it right now:**
1. Download ElevenReader from the App Store (free)
2. Find any long article, report, or document you've been putting off reading — copy the URL
3. Open ElevenReader, paste the URL in
4. Hit play and go do something else that doesn't require your eyes: walk the dog, do the dishes, commute

**Who it's most useful for:** Anyone with a growing reading backlog. If you have long newsletters, industry reports, or research papers piling up, this turns them into a podcast on demand. Simon Willison used it to get through the Pope's full AI encyclical on a dog walk — which is a good sign it can handle dense material.

## 💡 Prompt of the Week

**The "Audit My Context" Prompt — use this to quickly understand where you actually stand on any project or topic**

This technique comes from a workflow highlighted in The Rundown AI: using AI to audit what it knows about your work situation so you can spot gaps and misunderstandings before they become problems. It's particularly useful when starting a new project, preparing for a meeting, or handing work off to someone else.

Paste this into Claude or ChatGPT after you've shared relevant background about your project or situation:

```
Here is everything I've told you about [project/situation/topic]. 

Please do three things:
1. Summarize what you understand to be the current state of this work — the key facts, decisions made, and open questions.
2. Flag anything that seems contradictory, unclear, or that you're making assumptions about.
3. List the three most important things I haven't told you yet that would help you give me better advice.

Be direct. If your understanding is incomplete, say so clearly.
```

**When to use it:** Any time you've been going back and forth with an AI on a complex topic and you're not sure it has the full picture. Takes 30 seconds and regularly surfaces things you forgot to mention — or assumed the AI already knew.

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Turn a case study or past project into a client-ready video using AI — no video editing skills required**

If you work in consulting, marketing, sales, or any client-facing role, you probably have case studies sitting in documents that nobody reads. Here's how to turn one into a short video presentation using AI tools:

1. **Start with your case study document** — even a rough Word doc or Google Doc works
2. **Open Claude or ChatGPT** and paste in the text. Ask it: *"Turn this into a 90-second video script. Structure it as: problem → what we did → result. Use plain language, no jargon. Write it to be spoken out loud, not read."*
3. **Take that script to a tool like Runway, Synthesia, or HeyGen** — these let you paste a script and generate a video with an AI presenter, no camera required. Most have free tiers to try.
4. **Add your company logo or any relevant images** using the tool's editor
5. Send the video link instead of attaching a PDF next time a prospect asks for proof of work

The whole process can take under an hour. You're not replacing a professional video production — you're replacing a PDF that nobody opens.

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**SIGNAL: AI tools are starting to run on your behalf — not just answer questions. Here's why that matters for you now.**

The biggest shift happening in AI right now isn't a smarter chatbot. It's AI that takes actions, not just gives answers. This week, Google announced a series of updates at its I/O developer conference focused on "agentic" AI — meaning AI that can plan your day, browse the web, book things, and complete multi-step tasks while you go do something else.

Why does this matter to you *right now*, even if the full vision isn't here yet?

Because the tools you already use are quietly getting this capability added. Google's Gemini is being built into Gmail, Calendar, and Docs to handle tasks — not just draft text. The practical upshot: start paying attention to the "suggest," "automate," and "agent" features appearing in tools you already pay for. They're not gimmicks anymore. The people who figure out how to delegate routine tasks to these systems in the next six months will look like they have an extra assistant. The people who ignore it will wonder why some colleagues seem to get more done.

For now, the most practical version of this: look at the automation features in your current tools — Notion AI, Google Workspace AI, Microsoft Copilot — and pick *one* repetitive task to hand off this week.

**NOISE: The Microsoft Copilot security vulnerability**

This week, a security researcher found that Microsoft Copilot Cowork — a relatively new Microsoft product — had a vulnerability that could allow a malicious actor to access files from a user's OneDrive. Microsoft has been notified and these types of issues do get patched, but headlines like "AI tool can steal your files" are going to keep appearing as AI gets woven into workplace software.

Should you panic? Not yet. You almost certainly aren't using Copilot Cowork specifically (it's quite new and not widely adopted). But this is worth noting as a pattern to watch: as AI tools get access to your email, files, and calendar, security matters more. For now, the practical takeaway is simple — be thoughtful about which AI tools you grant access to sensitive files, and keep an eye on permission settings in whatever AI-enhanced software you use.

This is not an immediate crisis. It's a category of problem that will keep coming up, and awareness is more useful than alarm.
