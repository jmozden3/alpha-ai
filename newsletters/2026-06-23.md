## ⚡ The Alpha — Tip of the Week

**Use AI voice commands to cut your typing time — here's how to actually do it**

Most people think of AI as something you type at. But if your work involves moving between apps, composing messages on the go, or capturing thoughts mid-meeting, dictating to an AI instead of typing can quietly save you a meaningful chunk of your day.

Here's the workflow, no setup required:

1. **On your phone**, open the native keyboard and tap the microphone icon (it's built into both iPhone and Android keyboards). Speak naturally — punctuation, paragraph breaks, everything.
2. **Paste directly into ChatGPT or Claude** — or use the voice input built into those apps directly.
3. **Give AI a cleanup instruction**: say or type *"Clean this up — fix grammar, tighten it, keep my tone"* before or after your dictated text.

Where this really earns its keep: drafting a quick client email while walking back from a meeting, capturing notes right after a call while it's still fresh, or composing a Slack message that would have taken five minutes of pecking at your phone.

The friction point most people hit is perfectionism — they try to dictate a polished message and freeze. Don't. Dictate rough, let AI polish. It takes about two sessions to feel natural, and then you won't go back.

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**Gemini in Google Meet / Google Calendar — for meeting prep**

What it does: Gemini, built into Google Workspace, can read your calendar event details and generate a pre-meeting briefing — agenda, key context, suggested questions — before you walk in.

How to use it right now:
1. Open Google Calendar and click on an upcoming meeting.
2. If you have a Google Workspace account (the paid version used by most businesses), look for the Gemini or "meeting summary" prompt in the sidebar.
3. Type: *"Prepare me for this meeting — summarize the agenda, key attendees, and three things I should know or ask."*
4. If your company hasn't enabled it yet, paste the meeting invite text into gemini.google.com and ask the same question.

Best for: managers or consultants who jump between five meetings a day and currently walk into half of them underprepared.

## 💡 Prompt of the Week

**Turn a messy situation into a clear decision using the "Reddit complaint mining" frame**

This technique flips a common frustration — "I don't know what people actually want" — into real insight. Feed AI a description of your customer, client, or audience and ask it to surface the complaints they're probably not saying to your face.

Useful before writing a proposal, prepping a sales call, designing a new service, or figuring out why something you're offering isn't landing.

```
I serve [describe your customer or client type in one sentence].

Without me saying what I sell or offer, do the following:
1. List the 5 most common complaints, frustrations, or unmet needs this type of person likely has — the stuff they'd vent about anonymously online, not what they'd say in a survey.
2. For each complaint, write one sentence on what they've probably already tried and why it didn't work.
3. Flag which of these complaints they feel most urgently right now.

Be specific and direct. Don't soften the frustrations.
```

The goal is to surface the real problem before you pitch a solution — which almost always makes your pitch sharper.

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**SIGNAL: Norway just banned AI in elementary schools — and it matters beyond education**

Norway passed a near-total ban on AI use in elementary schools this week, citing concerns about children's learning development. If you work in education, training, or any role where you're helping people build skills (not just complete tasks), this is a useful nudge to think about *when* AI should do the work versus when the struggle *is* the point — a question that applies well beyond classrooms.
