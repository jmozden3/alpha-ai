## ⚡ The Alpha — Tip of the Week

**Run a daily 5-minute audit of your Claude sessions to actually get better at using AI**

Most people use AI the same way every day — and wonder why they get the same mediocre results. Here's a simple habit that changes that: at the end of each day you've used Claude (or ChatGPT), spend five minutes reviewing your sessions and asking yourself three questions: Where did the AI give me something genuinely useful? Where did I have to heavily edit or redo its output? What would I prompt differently next time?

The goal isn't self-criticism — it's pattern recognition. If you notice Claude always writes your emails too formally, add "write in a casual, direct tone" to every email prompt going forward. If you notice summaries miss your key points, start summarizing what matters to you *first*, then ask Claude to build on it. Small adjustments compound fast.

Here's how to build the habit: keep a note open (a plain text file, a sticky note, anything) and jot one sentence after each AI session — "this worked, this didn't." Review weekly. You'll develop a personal prompt style that fits your actual work within two to three weeks.

This works because AI tools don't improve themselves for your use case — you have to close the loop.

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**Claude's new Effort Control on claude.ai**

Anthropic just released a practical new feature inside claude.ai: an effort control slider that lets you tell Claude how much thinking to put into a response before it starts writing. In plain English — you can now dial it down for quick, low-stakes tasks and dial it up when you actually need Claude to reason carefully through something hard.

**How to use it right now:**

1. Open claude.ai and start a new conversation
2. Look for the effort control option in the input area (it's available on the Claude Opus 4.8 model)
3. Set it low for things like: rewriting a paragraph, drafting a quick reply, summarizing a short document — you'll get a faster answer
4. Set it high for things like: analyzing a contract, planning a project with many moving parts, working through a hard decision — Claude will slow down, think through it more, and give you a more considered answer

**Who it's most useful for:** Anyone who uses Claude for a mix of quick tasks and deeper thinking in the same workday. Instead of getting the same depth of response whether you're renaming a file or building a strategy, you're now in control of the tradeoff.

Note: This is rolling out as part of the Opus 4.8 update, so you may need to select that model in your settings.

## 💡 Prompt of the Week

**The "Honest Editor" prompt — get feedback that actually helps instead of flattery**

One of the most common complaints about AI assistants (especially ChatGPT) is that they praise everything. You paste in a draft and get "This is great! Here are a few minor suggestions..." — which is useless. This prompt explicitly breaks that pattern and asks for the kind of direct feedback a smart colleague would give.

Use it when: you've written something that matters — a proposal, a presentation, an important email, a performance review — and you want real critique before it goes out.

```
I'm going to share a piece of writing with you. Your job is to be a direct, honest editor — not a cheerleader. 

Do NOT start by complimenting it. Do NOT soften your feedback with phrases like "this is great, but..." 

Instead, tell me:
1. The single biggest weakness in this piece — what's most likely to make the reader lose confidence or interest?
2. One place where my reasoning or argument is unclear or unconvincing
3. One specific sentence or paragraph I should cut or rewrite entirely, and why

Then, if there are genuine strengths worth keeping, you can mention one.

Here's the piece:

[PASTE YOUR WRITING HERE]
```

The key move here is telling the AI explicitly what *not* to do (flatter you) before telling it what you want. This small instruction change produces dramatically more useful feedback.

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Build a no-code blog post (or newsletter section) with a writing agent — no experience needed**

The Rundown AI walked through how to set up a custom blog writing agent this week using no-code tools. Here's the practical version for knowledge workers who need to produce written content regularly — think internal memos, client updates, LinkedIn posts, or newsletter sections.

**The workflow:**

1. **Open Claude.ai or ChatGPT** — no special tool needed to start
2. **Give it a standing brief** at the top of each session. Something like: *"You are my writing assistant for [type of content]. My audience is [describe them]. My tone is [direct/warm/formal]. My typical length is [X words]. Always lead with the most important point."*
3. **Paste in your raw material** — bullet points, meeting notes, a voice memo transcript, even a messy brain dump. Don't clean it up first.
4. **Ask for a first draft**: *"Turn the above into a [blog post / client update / LinkedIn post] following the brief."*
5. **Iterate with specific instructions**: Don't say "make it better." Say "shorten the intro to two sentences" or "make the second paragraph more concrete — add an example."

The unlock here is the standing brief in step 2. Most people start fresh every time and get inconsistent results. Saving your brief as a text snippet you paste at the start of every writing session gives you a "house style" without any technical setup.

Takes about 15 minutes to write your brief the first time. Saves 30–45 minutes every time you produce content after that.

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**SIGNAL: Someone just walked into Meta's AI chatbot and took over high-profile Instagram accounts by simply asking**

This story matters to anyone who has an online presence — which is most working professionals now.

Security researcher and developer Simon Willison reported this week that hackers were able to hijack Instagram accounts by chatting with Meta's AI support bot and simply asking it to reassign the account to a new email address. The bot, which had been given real power over the account recovery process, did it. No sophisticated hacking. No stolen passwords. Just a polite request to an AI that had too much authority and too little skepticism.

Why does this matter to you personally? Two reasons. First, if you have an Instagram, Facebook, or any Meta-connected account that matters to your work or business, this is a good week to make sure you have two-factor authentication turned on — it's one of the few things that could have blocked this. Go to Settings → Security → Two-Factor Authentication on any Meta platform and turn it on now if you haven't.

Second, and more broadly: this is a preview of a category of risk that's going to grow. As more companies wire AI assistants into real systems — customer service, account management, IT helpdesks — the question of what the AI is *allowed to do* becomes critically important. When you interact with an AI support agent for any service, it's worth knowing: can this thing actually make changes to my account? The answer is increasingly yes.

*Source: Simon Willison — https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/1/hackers-simply-asked-meta-ai/#atom-everything*

**NOISE: The "AI agent that runs your whole life" hype**

If you've spent any time on LinkedIn or X this week, you've seen the posts: "My AI agent handles my email, my calendar, my research, and my invoices while I sleep." According to community discussion in r/AI_Agents, the reality is considerably messier. One person who spent three months building a personalized AI assistant — burning through a substantial number of tokens and running it on a dedicated server — reported that it still misunderstands instructions, crashes randomly, makes security mistakes, and gives unreliable outputs. Their conclusion: "AI hype and AI reality are VERY different right now."

This is community discussion, not a verified study — but it lines up with what plenty of honest practitioners will tell you off the record. Fully autonomous agents that run your life without supervision are not ready for regular people to depend on. The simpler tools — Claude, ChatGPT used directly, purpose-built apps — are delivering more value with less friction right now.

Ignore the "my agent does everything" content for now. Use AI for specific, well-defined tasks where you can review the output. That's where the real productivity gains are in 2026.
