## ⚡ The Alpha — Tip of the Week

**Use NotebookLM to stress-test a business decision before you commit to it.**

Most people use AI to generate ideas. Here's a better use: make it argue against you.

Here's how to do it with Google's NotebookLM (free at notebooklm.google.com):

1. **Upload your relevant documents** — a proposal, a business plan, a vendor quote, a strategy doc. Even a few pages of notes works.
2. **Ask it to play devil's advocate.** Try: *"You are a skeptical advisor. Based on these documents, what are the three biggest risks or weaknesses in this plan that I might be overlooking?"*
3. **Follow up with specifics.** *"What assumptions am I making that could be wrong? What would have to be true for this to fail?"*
4. **Ask for the counter-case.** *"Make the strongest argument against moving forward with this."*

What you get isn't a Magic 8-Ball — it's a structured pressure test built from your own materials. NotebookLM won't hallucinate facts from outside your documents; it works only with what you've uploaded, which makes the pushback actually relevant to your situation.

This works for any high-stakes decision: hiring someone, launching a product, signing a contract, pitching a client. Five minutes of AI-generated skepticism beats discovering the flaw after the fact.

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**Plaud — a wearable AI note-taker that captures meetings without your phone**

Plaud is a small physical device (it looks like a card) that records conversations and automatically generates summaries, action items, and transcripts. No laptop open, no awkward phone on the table.

How to use it right now:
1. Clip it on or set it near you before a meeting, call, or even a brainstorm session with yourself.
2. Hit record. That's it during the meeting — just be present.
3. Afterward, open the Plaud app to get a full transcript, a summary, and a list of action items already pulled out.

Best for: managers who run back-to-back meetings, consultants on client calls, or anyone who leaves a conversation with three pages of notes they'll never organize. The output is something you can paste directly into a follow-up email or project doc.

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Turn a wall of information into a decision-ready briefing in under ten minutes.**

If you've ever had to brief a boss, a board, or a client on a complicated topic — and spent an hour just figuring out what to include — this is for you.

1. Open ChatGPT (chatgpt.com) or Claude (claude.ai).
2. Paste in the raw material: an article, a report excerpt, a string of emails, meeting notes — whatever's relevant.
3. Type this prompt: *"I need to brief [my manager / board / client] on this in under five minutes. Summarize the key facts, flag the two or three things they most need to decide or know, and note anything that's still uncertain."*
4. Review the output, then follow up: *"Now write this as three short bullet points a non-expert would understand."*

The second prompt is where it earns its keep — it forces the AI to cut the jargon and prioritize. You'll have something you can actually send or say out loud, not a summary that reads like a Wikipedia article.
