## ⚡ The Alpha — Tip of the Week

**Before building an AI solution, run the "reorder point test" on your own work.**

Most of us have tasks that *feel* complex but are actually just repetitive decisions wearing complexity's clothes. The fix isn't AI — it's a simple rule. But some tasks genuinely need AI because the input is messy and unpredictable every single time. Knowing which is which saves you money, time, and a lot of frustration.

Here's how to do it before your next "AI project" — even if that project is just figuring out how to use AI better at work:

1. **List the task.** Write down exactly what you're trying to automate or improve with AI.
2. **Ask: is the input always roughly the same?** If yes — same type of request, same data, same decision each time — you probably don't need AI. You need a template, a checklist, or a simple rule ("if X, then Y").
3. **Ask: is the input unpredictable and messy?** If people are writing freeform requests, raising ambiguous issues, or combining multiple problems in one message, that's where AI earns its keep. It reads the mess and figures out what to do.
4. **Act accordingly.** For predictable tasks: build the simple version first (a saved email template, a spreadsheet trigger, a checklist). For messy tasks: bring in AI with a clear prompt describing what it should interpret and what it should output.

A concrete example of each: if you send the same project update email every Friday, a template beats AI every time. But if your inbox gets customer complaints that mix billing questions, product issues, and account changes in one paragraph — AI reading and sorting those is genuinely useful.

The goal isn't to use AI everywhere. It's to use the right tool and get your time back.

## 🛠 Tool of the Week

**Record & Replay (via The Rundown AI)**

A browser-based tool that watches you complete a manual task once, then automates it going forward — no code required.

**How to use it:**
1. Open the tool and hit Record.
2. Do the task you want automated — copying data between tabs, filling out a form, moving information from one place to another.
3. Stop recording. The tool captures every step.
4. Hit Replay whenever you need to run that task again.

**Best for:** Anyone who does the same repetitive browser task more than a few times a week — pulling data from one system into another, updating a spreadsheet from a web source, or filling out recurring forms.

No setup beyond a browser. Nothing to install. Nothing to configure.

## 🔓 Workflow Unlock

**Use AI to triage a messy inbox of similar requests — not to reply, just to sort.**

If you receive a recurring type of message (client questions, team requests, support emails) that all look different on the surface but fall into a handful of categories, AI can do the sorting for you in under a minute.

1. Copy 10–20 of those messages into ChatGPT or Claude.
2. Type: *"Read these messages and group them into categories based on the type of request. Label each one and explain the category in one sentence."*
3. Review the categories it produces. You'll almost certainly find 3–5 buckets you were mentally managing without realizing it.
4. Now ask: *"For each category, suggest a one-sentence rule I could use to handle it consistently."*

You've just turned a pile of ad-hoc decisions into a repeatable system — and you can build templates, delegation rules, or simple automations around it.

## 📡 Signal vs Noise

**SIGNAL:** Ford rehired experienced human quality inspectors after AI-based inspection systems fell short on the factory floor — a reminder that for high-stakes decisions (quality, safety, compliance), AI works best as a second opinion, not the final word. If you're using AI to review anything consequential, build in a human check before anything goes out the door.

*Source: Bloomberg via Hacker News — https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-25/ford-has-been-rehiring-quality-inspectors-after-ai-fell-short*

For the vast majority of knowledge workers, nothing has changed — the tools you're already using haven't gotten worse, and the new models aren't available to you yet. Check back in a few weeks.
